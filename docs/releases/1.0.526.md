# 1.0.526: range moves that survive slow members, restart recovery, production status

This version brings to `main` the work described in
[1.0.522-beta](1.0.522-beta.md), which was never released on its own, and the
crates.io packaging of [1.0.524](1.0.524.md) and [1.0.525](1.0.525.md). It
also fixes the two restart limits that 1.0.522-beta listed.

## Fixed

- **A slow member no longer strands a moved range.** Repeated decision
  requests are answered from what a member kept and use up nothing, and
  members serve decision and handoff steps for their own budget. Details in
  [1.0.522-beta](1.0.522-beta.md).
- **A range move stalled for good at `serving` on a slow host.** Every
  member works under its own fresh metadata observation, and the metadata
  leader bounded that observation by its connection's I/O timeout (2 s on a
  native host), whatever budget the member asked for. A member whose bridge
  preparation, publication or Open needed longer timed out on every
  repetition and never finished; the step's own longer budget did not help.
  The leader now serves a host metadata acquisition for the member's own
  budget, capped at the five seconds the protocol already allowed, and the
  member waits that long for the reply. A surviving member also takes a new
  observation for a preparation part when the one it holds is about to
  expire.
- **Bridge observation windows expired on a slow host.** A member (or the
  coordinator, for the activation record) collected one observation from
  every other new member one after the other, and the window held only
  while every observation was still fresh. Where one exchange took over a
  second, the first observation expired before the last arrived, and the
  same window failed on every repetition. The observations of one window are
  now collected at the same time; each is the unchanged authenticated
  exchange with the same checks.
- **Observation windows were a second shorter than their budget.** A
  member observed inside another member's publication, Open or release
  window answered from a metadata observation that ended a whole second
  before the window it had to fill: its step is served for its budget less
  a half-second reply margin, and the metadata leader took the same margin
  off the observation again. On a slow host the observing member's own
  durable write then never fit, and the same publication failed on every
  repetition. A bridge member now asks for its observations so that they
  last until its step or phase ends (the leader's margin added back, within
  the five-second cap), a bridge step asks for the full five seconds
  receivers allow, and a member's publication, Open or release phase lasts
  as long as its step.
  An observed member also reports the rest of its observation counted from
  the moment it began serving the request, not from its reply, so its frame
  no longer loses the time the member spent observing itself; the observer
  counts it from the moment its request began to be written, after the
  connection and its TLS handshake, which is still no later; a frame never
  outlives the member's observation.
- **A repeated bridge publication repeated a full check inside its
  window.** A member releases its own read pin once a complete window has
  observed every other member prepared; the release validates the member
  completely before and after, under its own service guard. A publication
  repeated after a later part failed released the already released pin
  again, paying both checks (about 0.7 s on a slow host) after the other
  members' observations had arrived, only to take nothing. A repetition now
  skips a release that already happened, as the in-process phase does.
- **Every two-member bridge phase was tried twice on a slow host.** The
  coordinator asked two members at once for a publication, Open, release or
  writer policy. Where the two could not observe each other while each was
  busy with its own step, they refused each other as busy and expired each
  other's windows, a two-member publication for 46 to 76 s, before each was
  repeated alone (about 3 s each). These steps, and the OLD custody
  retirement, which also reads every other member (three at once took 25 to
  37 s), now ask one member at a time; a fast host loses well under a second
  per phase.
- **A publication's own observation and its window now run together.** A
  member took its own pre-quorum observation (up to 2 s on the CI runner)
  before it asked the other members for theirs, and their frames then ran
  out before its durable write. Both are pre-quorum observations of the same
  round, which the later post-quorum observation must follow, and no order
  is required between them; they are now taken at the same time, and so
  are a writer policy's own release observation and its window. A
  repetition also reads whether its read pin was already released under the
  service guard, instead of refusing when the pin slot was held at that
  instant ("pin transition busy").
- **A Strong read competed with its own second exchange.** A client keeps
  one further exchange in flight beside a read that is slow to answer, and a
  member busy with the first read of the range refuses that second read
  (the first one holds the range's exclusive read context). The second read
  used to take a host metadata observation and a leader metadata
  observation, a quorum round on the metadata leader each, before it was
  refused; under load that work slowed the first read past its budget, and
  on a slow runner one read was repeated for 22 s. A member now refuses
  such a read before its metadata acquisitions, with the same text. With
  `BICDB_RANGE_MOVE_TRACE` set, a Strong read that takes more than half a
  second reports the time of each of its steps.
- **Strong reads and leader recovery locked each other out after a
  restart.** A Strong read took its range's exclusive read context first
  and then waited for the range's admission. When a leader recovery held
  that admission, the recovery's own local receiver step needed the same
  context, was refused, and retried, by which time the next read held the
  context again. After a whole-cluster restart no Strong read of the range
  got through for 22 s, each refused while its own first exchange held the
  context. The read now takes the context only once the range is admitted,
  the same order recovery takes them in; the server-side read was never
  orphaned, as it ends at its own request deadline.
- **A durable bridge was refused after it was written.** A publishing
  member's first window had to stay live until the end of its native
  publication, including the proofs it runs after the durable write. On the
  CI runner those took about a second; the frames ran out after the write,
  the publication was refused, and every repetition recovered the same
  bridge and ran out again. The frames are now checked immediately before
  the journal commit, inside its transaction, and not after it: what they
  attested decides only whether to write, and the publication stays
  unconfirmed until a second window, observed after the write, reports
  every other member unchanged.
- **A durable catalogue activation was held to the same window.** The Open
  step had the same shape: its first window had to stay live through the
  whole local activation write, including the proofs after it. The frames
  are now checked immediately before the activation is written, or at the
  decision to recover an earlier attempt's exact activation, and the second
  window confirms the activation as before. The member's own fresh
  observations still bound every check after the write. The member's own
  pre-quorum Open observation is now taken at the same time as the window,
  as publication does, instead of before it; on the CI runner it took about
  0.7 s, and the frames of one member then ran out before the commit point
  on every repetition. Writer and domain proofs whose results were thrown
  away (a re-fsync check with nothing to re-fsync, a recovery check with
  nothing to recover) are no longer computed; the write still runs them
  before anything is written.
- **A source discovery refused on a momentary metadata lock.** A donor
  serving a source discovery or decision provenance request refused with
  "source discovery metadata busy" when its own metadata store was held at
  that instant, for example by a supervisor tick. It now waits for the store
  while the request and its own observation are live.
- **A refused writer policy publication could never be repeated.** A
  surviving member marked its Serving writer policy record as written
  before the checks that end its transaction. When one of them refused (an
  expired observation window, on a slow host), the transaction was rolled
  back, but every later attempt took the record as possibly written and
  refused with "writer Serving audit accounting regressed". A record is now
  marked uncertain only once its transaction reaches its commit; a refused
  attempt leaves nothing behind and the next one writes afresh.
- **Strong reads slowed down with every write a node had prepared.** Each
  read re-read and re-checksummed the whole on-disk range catalogue once per
  abort slot, so its cost grew with the square of the node's write history. On
  a slow host after restarts, no read could collect a quorum within its
  budget. A read now checks the catalogue identity once per check and
  validates every slot and promise record against it; no record check is
  dropped. A refused Strong read now names the holder of a busy recovery
  context and the time each step took.
- **Restarting a range leader killed mid-write.** The restarted leader now
  resolves its unfinished write in every kill window the test reaches:
  - A write that was only prepared, or only started, is **aborted**. A new
    record proves the write had no effect, and only for a slot an earlier
    process left. The leader marks a write for commit, durably, before Core
    writes anything and before any Commit is sent; such a slot is never
    aborted.
  - A write the leader had marked for commit or voted to commit, but not yet
    sent, is **finished**: the leader re-sends its own Commit round inside
    the fenced recovery, and followers vote through their unchanged checks.
  - A write the leader had already applied locally is **recorded** from its
    own certified entry.
  - Any other shape is refused by name in the status.
  - A client is only ever told that such a write's outcome is unknown; the
    hint now says to re-read once the range accepts writes again.
  - An independent review found no way to lose or invent a committed write.
- **Restarting a follower after a hot recovery.** The range leader runs a
  fresh re-arm session when a member holds an activation its new process
  never armed, so the restarted follower serves reads and votes again.

## Changed

- **Range move trace.** With `BICDB_RANGE_MOVE_TRACE` set, a member now
  reports each part of its bridge preparation, and an expired observation
  window reports how much of each of its bounds was left when the
  observation arrived.
- **Production status.** The README and every crates.io package now say:
  BicDB is production software, not an experiment. It runs a search cluster
  with 2.1 billion records, health-record backends and field apps. The
  "Beta" labels are removed.

## Limits

- A follower restarted more than one write behind its range cannot catch up
  through the restart recovery; that range waits for it.
- A member crash during a range move, after the source cut, leaves that
  range unavailable (no data is lost). `serve-reopen` is refused while a move
  is unfinished.
- There is no automatic range-leader failover.
- The other limits of [1.0.522-beta](1.0.522-beta.md) still apply.

## Validation

All runs on the shared build host, lightly optimized test build, serial, on
the integrated branch:

- **Core suites:** 71 passed, 0 failed; 59 passed, 0 failed.
- **Restart CLI test (four processes):** 2 passed, 0 failed, with the whole
  test limited to half of one CPU. In earlier runs, 9 of 9 leader kills
  under writes recovered.
- **Six-process move test:** passed with the test limited to half of one CPU.
- **Operator command suite:** 6 passed, 0 failed.
