# 1.0.522-beta: range moves that survive slow members, a second node after a move, restartable initial members

1.0.521-beta was merged with its slow-runner check red. That check found a
real defect, fixed here first: a slow member could leave a moved range
unavailable for good. This release also removes three of 1.0.521-beta's
limits: no further node after a move, no second move, no restart of an
initial member.

## Fixed

- **A slow member no longer strands the moved range.** On a slow host a
  member's decision preparation ran past the coordinator's 2 s network
  deadline. It finished after the reply was lost, and each repetition used
  one of the member's eight lifetime preparation attempts. With the eighth,
  the decision stage refused permanently. That happened after the source
  cut, where a move cannot be aborted.
  - A repeated preparation or publication for the same decision is now
    answered from what the member kept, and uses nothing new.
  - A request refused before it changed anything leaves nothing behind.
  - A repetition arriving while the earlier request still runs is refused as
    busy.
  - Members' request tables (decision participants, the source's discovery
    and export retirement, the target's export observation) hold a request
    only while its own budget runs.
  - A member's single durable decision write is not begun with too little
    budget left to finish it.
  - Decision requests and handoff steps are served for their own budget (at
    most 5 s) instead of the 2 s network deadline. That deadline also cut off
    bridge publication and Open on a slow host, on every repetition.
- **Range moves are faster.** The coordinator sends the member steps of one
  activation phase together, at most two or three members at once. Every
  ordering the protocol needs is kept: phases in sequence, the target first
  at Open and release, the target last at writer policy, every preparation
  before any publication. A move between five processes now takes about
  14 s, with the range refused for about 12 to 15 s (before: about 18 s).
- **Write hold.** The coordinator keeps the hold around the learner
  allocation until it has published the changed range, also when the
  commit's outcome is unknown, for at most 10 s. A timeout, or a coordinator
  without a write hold, is reported in the move status `notes`.
- **Refusal on an unfinished recovery.** It now names the voter, the record,
  its phase, its fencer, the fenced epoch and the voter's log epoch.

## Added

- **A further node after a completed move.**
  - **Before:** after one completed move, a node joining as a metadata voter
    made every range refuse Strong reads and appends on every member.
  - **Member side:** a member's writer registry successor now accepts
    exactly one kind of range change. That change is a handoff the member
    verifies from its own committed metadata: relocation `Completed`, stage
    `Cleaned`, certified scope present, epochs chained, exactly one per
    epoch. Nothing is taken from a request or a reply.
  - **What the successor does not change:** it grants no write authority.
    Writes are still checked against the enrolled policy or the member's own
    serving audit.
  - **Metadata leader:** it refuses a further voter while a recorded
    relocation is one the members cannot follow. The refusal commits
    nothing.
  - **Verified:** between six processes, the sixth node joins after the
    move, all four ranges keep serving, and a second range is moved onto it.
- **Initial members can restart.** `serve-reopen` reopens an initial
  member's own root.
  - The member rejoins metadata as a follower and gets a new writer policy.
  - It takes back the ranges it leads through the ordinary leader recovery,
    which needs a current quorum of the range's voters.
  - A write that its log certifies committed but that was not applied is
    finished by the hot recovery.
  - Nothing the old process held in memory is reused.
  - A four-process test kills a follower, a range leader, the metadata
    leader and then all four members, and reads back every acknowledged
    write.
- **Move refused before anything is committed** when a voter's writer
  policy does not cover the committed membership.

## Limits

- **The moved range is unavailable during the move,** from the learner
  allocation until the target serves: about 12 to 15 s between five
  processes. No acknowledged write was lost.
- **Restarting a range leader after a crash mid-write.** If the crashed
  leader's last write was only prepared, its range keeps refusing writes and
  reads after the restart. No acknowledged write is lost, and the status
  names the range. A host-driven abort for this case is in review and is not
  part of this release.
- **Restart during a move.** `serve-reopen` is refused while any move is
  unfinished. A member crash after a move's source cut is not survivable for
  that range: no data is lost, but the range stays unavailable.
- **No range-leader failover.** A range whose leader is down refuses writes
  until that member is back.
- **Very slow hosts.** On a host where all processes share 40% of one CPU,
  the move can stall at the activation record. With 50% it completed in
  three of three runs.
- **No further node after a failed move.** A `Failed` relocation, or a
  completed one without its certified scope, refuses every further metadata
  voter. The refusal is safe, but there is no recovery path yet.
- **What a target must be.** The target must hold no range.
- **What a coordinator must be.** It must be both the metadata leader and
  the leader of the moved range. A node that joined through a move cannot
  coordinate a move or be an old member of one, so the same range cannot be
  moved a second time.
- **Other limits.**
  - A member that is down during the decision round stalls the move.
  - Nothing after the cut can be aborted.
  - Retired source rows are not removed.
  - Do not mix versions in one cluster.

## Validation

All on the shared build host, lightly optimized test build, serial runs of
the integrated branch:

- **Core tests:**
  - move, backend, decision, certificate, hold and recovery-loader tests: 47
    passed, 0 failed;
  - bridge and handoff steps, fifth-node path, completion, initial member
    restart, hot recovery and writer registry tests: 59 passed, 0 failed;
  - in-process driver policy tests: 36 passed, 0 failed.
- **Six-process acceptance test:** passed twice, and once with the whole test
  limited to half of one CPU.
  - First move: about 14 s, range refused 12 to 14 s.
  - The sixth node then joined with 0 of 4 ranges refusing.
  - A second range was moved onto the sixth node.
  - Every acknowledged append was read back.
- **CLI restart test (four processes):** 2 passed, 0 failed, on its own branch before integration.
- **Operator command suite:** 6 passed, 0 failed.
