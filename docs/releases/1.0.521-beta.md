# 1.0.521-beta: a range move across five separate processes

1.0.520-beta is reserved by a parallel candidate and is not used here.

1.0.519-beta added the first pieces of a cross-process range handoff. This
release completes the route: one operator command moves a replicated range
onto a fifth node while every member runs in its own process. It is an early
operator feature with real limits, listed below. Read them before using it.

## What is added

- **Operator commands.** `move-range` starts a move and `transfer-metadata-leadership`
  makes the metadata leader coincide with the range leader, which a move
  requires. The host status reports the move's stage, what it is waiting for
  and, when blocked, the member responsible.
- **Remote backend, complete.** The coordinator drives every stage over
  authenticated requests: readiness check of all members, source cut, image
  install on the target, decision round, the three membership commits,
  bridge preparation, publication and opening, custody retirement, release,
  writer policy, target serving, activation record and source retirement.
  One call walks a move to `Completed`; a restarted coordinator resumes from
  committed metadata.
- **Member steps.** Each member performs its own local work when asked,
  under its own freshly acquired role and its own reading of metadata.
  Requests carry identities only; no role, quorum or certificate is accepted
  from a request.
- **Certified scope in the committed record** (record version 2), so the
  promotion and final membership commits no longer need the decision proof
  to be re-collected after a restart.
- **Five-process acceptance test** in the CLI test suite: five hosts, a
  client appending and reading the moved range, the move to completion, and
  a check that every acknowledged append is readable afterwards.

## Review fixes included

- A decision is certified only when every new member and the retiring source
  hold it durably. A majority-only round could have stranded the range.
- All members are asked for readiness before the learner allocation is
  committed, and an abort before the cut works for a remote coordinator.
- An abort no longer reports "nothing committed" while a proposal of this
  host is undecided.
- A leadership transfer is refused while this host's move is unfinished.
- A reused or skipped operator sequence number is refused without ending the
  host; errors of a move step are reported.
- Members bind a step to the certified scope before keeping anything, release
  finished entries, and report "released" only after confirmation.

- A range that had been through a hot recovery would have been left
  unloadable by its next move: the source cut advanced the log epoch past
  the kept recovery record and the catalogue loader refused the file. The
  loader now accepts exactly that case (an activated recovery superseded by
  a durable promise of the same session, log at the promise's activation
  epoch), and a cut is verified before it is written. An independent review
  found no way to turn this into wrong data or revived old authority.
- Member steps no longer answer "not complete" on a timing edge under client
  traffic; the coordinator repeats a busy member by time, not by count, and
  starts the next stage without waiting for a supervisor tick.
- A slow member could leave the moved range unavailable for good. A member
  that needed longer for its decision preparation than the coordinator's
  network deadline completed the work after the reply was lost; every
  repetition then used one of that member's eight preparation attempts, and
  with the eighth the decision stage refused permanently, after the source
  cut, where a move cannot be aborted. A repeated preparation or publication
  for the same decision is now answered from what the member retained and
  takes nothing new; a request that was refused before it changed anything
  leaves nothing behind; a repetition that arrives while the earlier request
  still runs is refused as busy instead of doing the work twice. The request
  tables of the decision participants, of the source's discovery and export
  retirement and of the target's export observation now hold a request only
  while its own budget runs, so repetition over time cannot use them up.
  The coordinator repeats the decision publication by time while members are
  only slow or busy. A member's one durable decision write, which is never
  replaced, is not begun when its request has too little budget left to
  finish it; the repetition begins it instead.
- A member was given at most the network deadline (2 s) for a decision
  request or a handoff step, whatever budget the request carried. On a slow
  host a bridge publication or Open needed longer and was cut off on every
  repetition, so the move never reached `serving`. These two requests are
  now served for their own budget (at most 5 s), and the coordinator waits
  for it.

## Limits

- **The moved range is unavailable during the move.** Strong reads and writes
  on it are refused from the learner allocation until the target serves:
  16 to 20 seconds between five processes on an otherwise idle host, 28 to
  34 seconds on a busy one. No acknowledged write was lost. The allocation
  advances the range epoch, so the old voters cannot keep serving up to the
  cut without a protocol change.
- **Writes at the start of a move.** The coordinator holds new writes on the
  range for a moment, lets one in flight finish and then commits the
  allocation. If the outcome of that commit is unknown the hold ends early;
  a write caught by it makes the coordinator refuse the cut, and the move
  must be aborted and started again. The hold exists only when the
  coordinator is an initial data owner.
- A source cut that fails on one member after another member kept its
  promise can only be resumed, not aborted. The known causes are refused
  before the cut.
- Nothing after the source cut can be aborted, and a member crash after the
  cut is not survivable for that range (no data is lost; the range stays
  unavailable). Original members cannot restart; only learner hosts can.
- A member that is down during the decision round stalls the move.
- On a starved host (all five processes sharing 40% of one CPU, with client
  traffic on the moved range) the move can stall at the activation record:
  the coordinator needs an observation of every new member inside one short
  window and does not get one while the members answer slowly or busy. No
  data is lost; the range stays unavailable. With half of one CPU the move
  completed in each of three runs.
- The coordinator must be both the metadata leader and the leader (and
  retiring source) of the moved range.
- The same range cannot be moved a second time.
- Retired source rows are not physically removed.
- The new requests and record version 2 are not compatible with older
  binaries; do not mix versions in one cluster.

## Validation

- Shared build host, lightly optimized test build, integrated branch, serial
  runs: move, backend, step, certificate, completion and hold tests 59
  passed, 0 failed; in-process driver policy tests 36 passed, 0 failed;
  remote member tests 139 passed, 0 failed; operator command suite 6 passed,
  0 failed.
- Five-process acceptance test: see the pull request for the final runs.
- A test fixture counted a read that the client itself had cancelled as a
  malformed evidence record, which failed two full-walk tests intermittently.
  The fixture now classifies exactly that record; every other incomplete
  record and any gap still fails.
